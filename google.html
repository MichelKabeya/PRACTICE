The Last Light of Kanyisa

The rain had been falling for three days. Not the gentle kind of rain that tapped against windows and disappeared into the soil almost as quickly as it came. This rain came down heavily, turning the narrow roads into rivers of brown mud and making the hills disappear behind curtains of grey water. The trees bent beneath the wind, their branches scratching against rooftops, while thunder rolled across the valley like enormous stones being dragged across the sky. At the edge of the valley stood a small village called Kanyisa. Kanyisa was an old place. It had been there for generations, surrounded by mountains on three sides and a deep forest on the fourth. During the day, the village looked peaceful. Small houses with metal roofs stood between vegetable gardens and fruit trees. Chickens wandered between fences. Children played football on a patch of dry ground near the community hall. But at night, Kanyisa became different. The mountains seemed taller. The forest seemed closer. And when the wind moved through the trees, the villagers often said it sounded like someone whispering their names. There was one place in Kanyisa that everyone knew about but almost nobody visited. At the top of the northern mountain stood an abandoned lighthouse. It was strange because Kanyisa was nowhere near the ocean. The lighthouse had been built almost a hundred years earlier by a man named Elias Venter, a railway engineer who had disappeared under mysterious circumstances. Nobody knew exactly why he had built it. Some said he had been obsessed with finding his way home during the storms. Others claimed he had discovered something inside the mountain and built the tower to mark the location. Whatever the truth was, the lighthouse had been abandoned for decades. Except for one thing. Every year, on the last night of winter, its light appeared. Nobody knew who turned it on. Nobody had climbed the mountain in years. Yet every winter, at exactly midnight, a pale golden light shone from the top of the tower. The villagers called it the Last Light. And they had a rule. Never follow it.
